Build the story of the video: a list of parts of the stream that stay in the video.

Rules:
1. Strictly in stream chronology: parts go in increasing time and do not overlap. The video is the stream itself, only shorter: the viewer must understand what happened from start to end (how it began, the main events, how it ended).
2. The backbone is the best moments (strong/good), but not necessarily all of them. Between them come linking parts (role "context"): the setup of a joke, what is happening in the game or the conversation, the start of a fight before the win, the cause of an argument. Without context the climax does not work.
   maybe and even skip episodes can serve as context if the story is unclear without them.
3. The total length of all parts in the source is at most 28 minutes, so that with the intro the finished video fits into 30 minutes. There is no minimum: if there are 5 minutes of real material, use 5 minutes. Do not stretch the story for length.
4. Each part is from 15 seconds to 4 minutes. Start at the beginning of a phrase or thought, end after the punchline or reaction (leave 2–3 seconds after the last word for a reaction).
5. 5–9 chapters, each with a short name (2–6 words) in the language of the stream's speech, truthful, no clickbait: these are YouTube chapters.
6. Do not take: the placeholder screen and music at the start of the stream, segments muted by the platform (list below), sponsored integrations and ad reads, long silence, conversations that make no sense without the picture (unless an in-game event is itself the climax — then pace "loose"), politics and anything dangerous for the channel.
7. pace: "tight" — conversation, cut pauses hard; "loose" — gameplay or action where what happens on screen matters (a boss fight, a run), keep pauses.
8. peaks: 0–3 timestamps inside a part where the streamer shows a strong emotion (a shout, laughter, shock, rage, the joy of a win) — at these seconds the edit zooms into the webcam. Do not put peaks on calm speech.
9. music: true if music is audible in the part (the streamer plays a track, sings, watches a music video) — a copyright-match risk.
10. title: the title of the whole video in the language of the stream's speech, up to 90 characters, starting with the streamer's nickname and a colon, the intrigue of the stream without lying. hook: one or two sentences for the description, in the same language, about what this stream was about.
